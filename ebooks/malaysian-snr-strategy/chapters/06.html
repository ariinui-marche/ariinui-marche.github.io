CHAPTER 6: Continuation Setups – Riding the Momentum

You’ve missed the early move? No worries. Continuation is where consistency begins.

6.1 The Role of Storyline Parts in Continuation

Let’s take a step back and break things down in the clearest way possible.

We’ve learned that market direction is formed in three parts within the weekly and daily storylines.

•The first part:

has its own structure and key levels, which we use to build specific setups.

•The last two parts :form a trend that continues the existing direction.

• This trend creates specific levels, which become the foundation for our Continuation Setups.

The Simple Rule

•In the first part of the weekly/daily storyline → We take specific setups related to its structure.

•In the last two parts → We take Continuation Setups, using the trend’s key levels.

Why This Matters

By understanding which phase of the storyline we are in, we can:

✔ Use the right setups at the right time instead of randomly entering trades.

✔ Maximize trade accuracy by focusing on structured setups rather than noise.

✔ Follow the flow of the market without forcing trades against the trend.

Now, let’s dive deeper into how we identify and execute these continuation setups effectively.

6.2.Types of Levels in Each Part of the Storyline

Every direction on the weekly or daily chart follows a three-part structure—the first part forms a QM pattern with its own levels, and the last two parts create a continuation trend with different levels. Here’s how it breaks down: 1- First Part: QM Pattern Formation

•Key Levels:

• GAP

• LS (Left Shoulder)

• SBR or RBS (depending on bearish or bullish direction)

• Apex

These levels come together to form a QM pattern, setting the foundation for the new direction. We’ve already explored the possible setups that arise from these QM levels (e.g., apex entries, gap entries, LS entries).

Last Two Parts: Continuation Trend

•Key Levels:

• C.APEX

• GAP

• SBR or RBS

Once the QM pattern initiates the trend, these continuation levels confirm that the market is carrying on in the same direction. Each level—C.APEX, gap, and SBR/RBS—has its own unique continuation setups, allowing you to ride the trend until it naturally completes.

Putting It All Together

•Part 1 (QM Pattern): GAP, LS, SBR/RBS, Apex

•Parts 2 & 3 (Continuation Trend): C.APEX, GAP, SBR/RBS

By recognizing which part of the storyline you’re in and which levels are forming, you can adapt your strategy—using QM-based setups in the first part and continuation setups in the last two parts. This approach ensures you capture both the initial reversal and the ongoing trend, giving you a comprehensive way to trade from start to finish.

6.3.Continuation Setups: Riding the Middle & End of the Storyline Once the first part of a storyline kicks off with a QMS setup, the market moves into the last two parts, where Continuation Setups shine. In these phases, the C.APEX, GAP, and SBR/RBS levels guide us to ride the ongoing trend.

At the same time, we refine our entries by leveraging mini storylines (4H → 1H → 30M → 15M).

Continuation setups are used to confirm and ride the existing trend after a QMS entry. They are found in the last two parts of the storyline on any timeframe.

•Middle and last parts of storyline → Continuation setups

6.4.Diving Deeper into Continuation Setups in Mini Storylines (4H to 15M) Now, let’s break down Continuation Setups within the mini storylines on the 4H to 15M timeframes by understanding the types of levels we’ll find in each phase.

Mini Storylines: A Smaller Version of the Bigger Picture

The mini storyline (4H to 15M) follows the exact same structure as the weekly and daily storylines, just on a smaller scale.

•The first part of the mini storyline contains a structural shift, forming an initial QM pattern with its key levels.

•The last two parts represent the continuation of that direction, creating a trend that follows the move initiated by the first phase.

•These continuation phases generate trend-following levels, such as:

• Continuation APEX (C.APEX)

• Gaps

• SBR/RBS levels

Since each timeframe nests within another, the mini storylines act like zoomed-in versions of the higher timeframe storylines.

Think of it as a picture inside a picture inside a picture—a fractal-like structure where each part reflects the same market behavior at different scales.

6.5.Multi-Timeframe (MTF) Mini Storyline Phases Inside the Storyline The multi-timeframe (MTF) storyline follows a structured pattern from higher timeframes (HTF) to lower timeframes (LTF). Each HTF storyline contains mini storylines that mirror the same three-phase structure but on a smaller scale.

1- MTF Flow: Higher Timeframe (HTF) → Lower Timeframe (LTF)

The MTF storyline structure works as follows:

•The higher timeframe (HTF) sets the major storyline.

•Inside the last two parts of that HTF storyline, smaller mini storylines appear.

•These mini storylines repeat at lower timeframes (LTF), following the same three-phase cycle.

2- Breakdown of the MTF Mini Storylines Inside the Last Two Parts of Each HTF Storyline

📌 Weekly Storyline → Contains 4H Mini Storylines

•Inside the last two phases of the weekly storyline, only 4H mini storylines are found.

•This means that when the weekly trend continues, the market structure inside that trend is mainly visible on the 4H timeframe.

📌 Daily Storyline → Contains 4H or 1H Mini Storylines

•The daily storyline usually starts with a 1H or 4H QM pattern.

•Therefore, inside its last two phases, where the trend continues, the 4H or 1H mini storylines structure the movement.

📌 4H Mini Storyline → Contains 1H, 30M, and 15M Mini Storylines

•The 4H mini storyline starts with a 1H, 30M, or 15M QM pattern.

•So, inside its last two phases, where the trend continues, the 1H, 30M, or 15M mini storylines take shape.

📌 1H Mini Storyline → Contains 30M and 15M Mini Storylines

•The 1H mini storyline starts with a 30M or 15M QM pattern.

•So, inside its last two phases, where the trend continues, the 30M or 15M mini storylines appear.

📌 30M & 15M Mini Storylines → The Last Stop

•The first part of the 30M & 15M mini storylines is the last stop in this fractal breakdown.

•In their last two phases, only 5M and 1M trends are found—there are no mini storylines beyond this point.

•There is no mini storyline for the 5M or 1M timeframes.

6.8.MTF Continuation Setups: Using 4H and 1H SNR Levels

Now, let’s dive into the second type of continuation setups—those based on the 4H and 1H SNR levels. These setups have simple rules that must be followed to confirm your trade entry using lower timeframe (LTF) confluence. Later, we’ll cover the specific confirmation types, but for now, let’s focus on the SNR level alignment.

1. 4H SNR Level Continuation Setups

•4H SNR Levels can be:

• A/V Shape Levels

• Bullish or Bearish Gap Levels

However, you shouldn’t take your trade directly from the 4H level without LTF confirmation. The rule is: The 4H level must align with lower timeframes—from 1H down to 30M and 15M.

In other words, regardless of whether the 4H level is an A/V shape or a gap level, it needs to be confirmed by corresponding levels on the 1H timeframe and/or the 30M/15M levels. When the 4H level aligns with one of these LTF levels, you can take your trade directly, pending the appropriate confirmation type.

2. 1H SNR Level Continuation Setups

•1H SNR Levels can also be:

• A/V Shape Levels

• Bullish or Bearish Gap Levels

Similarly, you shouldn’t enter a trade solely based on the 1H level. The 1H level must align with lower timeframes—

from 30M down to 15M. That means no matter which type (A/V or gap), the 1H level must be confirmed by the 30M/15M A/V shape levels. When the 1H level aligns with one of these, you’re ready to trade, again with your confirmation type to be used later.

Key Concepts

•Continuation SNR Setups:

These setups capture the early continuation of the trend. They are the levels where the price, after the initial QM

pattern, shows renewed movement before the mini storyline develops further.

•4H SNR Levels for Mini Storyline:

When a 4H SNR level provides a strong continuation signal, it acts as the starting point for the 4H mini storyline.

• This level is where you expect the trend to resume on the 4H chart, and it forms the basis for further entries and trade management in the mini storyline.

•1H SNR Levels for Mini Storyline:

Similarly, a 1H SNR level that shows continuation potential will serve as the launchpad for the 1H mini storyline.

• Here, the trend’s early continuation is confirmed on the 1H chart, setting up precise entries for the mini timeframe.

How It Works

1.Early Continuation Signal:

1. Before the mini storyline fully develops, the market often tests a key SNR level.

2. If the price respects this level and then starts moving in the same direction, that level is taken as a continuation SNR level.

2.Starting the Mini Storyline:

1. For 4H: The 4H SNR level, once confirmed as a continuation signal, marks the beginning of the 4H mini storyline.

2. For 1H: The 1H SNR level that shows continuation becomes the starting point for the 1H mini storyline.

1. These continuation setups allow you to enter early—capturing the renewed move before the mini storyline unfolds fully.

•Rule:

Always use at least 30 pips as your SL.

•Note:

You can use more than 30 pips if necessary, but never less.

•Purpose:

This rule ensures you place your SL in a "safe zone" to protect against minor fluctuations—even though no true

“safe zone” exists, this concept gives you a buffer against market noise.

Take Profit (TP) Guidelines

1. TP at the Next Fresh Level or Trendline

•Rule: Your TP should always be placed at the next fresh level or trendline—especially when the mini-storyline aligns with the daily storyline.

•Purpose: This ensures that your exit point is based on a logical, observable market structure.

2. Fixed TP with a 1:2 or 1:3 Risk-to-Reward Ratio

•Rule: Alternatively, you can use a fixed TP based on a 1:2 or 1:3 risk-to-reward (RR) ratio.

•Purpose: Lower timeframes can be noisy, so having a fixed RR helps avoid premature exits and prevents trades from reaching breakeven.

3. TP at the End of the Mini Storyline

•Rule: Set TP at the end of the third part of the mini-storyline.

• If the mini-storyline has reached its natural end and a reversal is beginning, place TP accordingly.

• If it hasn’t ended, adjust TP higher or lower based on the prevailing trend.

Inside the Storyline Phases:

•First Part (Weekly & Daily):

This phase is ideal for Swing and Intraday trading. Here, you capture broader market moves and trend shifts that align with longer-term structures. In this phase, setups are designed to take advantage of significant swing moves, offering a clear directional bias over a longer period.

•Last Two Parts (Weekly & Daily):

In these later phases, the market becomes more dynamic. Setups here are geared toward Intraday and Scalping strategies. These setups are designed for rapid entries and exits, taking advantage of quick, short-term moves. This phase is perfect for traders looking to capitalize on fast, precise movements in the market.
