4.1.The Storyline Concept in Trading

What is the Storyline in Trading?

The storyline in trading is the logical flow of price between SNR levels and trendlines across different timeframes. It explains why price moves, where it is heading, and how we can anticipate its next move.

Why is the Storyline Important?

✅ Removes randomness → The market is structured, not chaotic.

✅ Improves accuracy → You predict movements instead of reacting late.

✅ Aligns timeframes → Ensures smaller trends fit into the bigger picture.

📌 Example – The Market as a Movie 🎬:

•SNR levels = Key scenes → Price moves from one to another.

•Trendlines = Story direction → They guide price like a plotline.

•Breakouts & rejections = Plot twists → They change price behavior.

•Timeframes = Camera angles → Each timeframe shows the same story differently.

Master the storyline, and you master the market.

The Classic Storyline – How Price Naturally Moves:

The Classic Storyline follows a structured path as price moves between support, resistance: A. Beginning Phase – The Initial Move

•Price starts from a fresh SNR level .

•It follows the trend until it reaches another key level.

B. Decision Phase – Breakout or Rejection?

•Price touches an SNR level .

•Two possible outcomes:

🔹 Rejection → Price bounces back.

🔹 Breakout → Price continues to the next level.

C. Confirmation Phase – The Next Move

•If price breaks out, it retests the level before continuing.

•If price rejects, it forms a new setup and reverses

📌 Example – The Market as a Basketball Game 🏀:

•SNR levels = Hoops → Price moves from one to another.

•Trendlines = Court boundaries → They keep price in play.

•Rejections = Missed shots → Price bounces off resistance.

•Breakouts = Scoring a basket → Price moves to the next level.

This cycle repeats on all timeframes, forming a predictable Classic Storyline.

•This ensures we are not trading against the overall market structure.

4.7.Monthly Advanced Storyline

When fresh SNR levels on the monthly timeframe are no longer available, the Monthly Advanced Storyline takes over. This approach relies on trendlines and multi-timeframe breakout confirmations to define the long-term market direction.

1. How the Monthly Advanced Storyline Is Defined

•Foundation on Trendlines:

The advanced storyline is built on the monthly trendline rather than fresh SNR levels. This means we use the structure provided by the monthly trendline to determine the market direction.

•Breakout Confirmation:

The change in the monthly advanced storyline is confirmed when a daily breakout occurs from the monthly trendline:

• Bearish Monthly Advanced Storyline: Begins when the daily trend shifts from bullish to bearish via a bearish daily breakout at a monthly trendline.

• Bullish Monthly Advanced Storyline: Begins when the daily trend shifts from bearish to bullish via a bullish daily breakout at a monthly trendline.

•Weekly Breakout Possibility:

In some cases, a weekly breakout (on a fresh weekly level that aligns with the monthly trendline) can occur before the daily breakout and still confirm the monthly advanced direction.

2. Key Points of the Monthly Advanced Storyline

•Trendline-Based Structure:

Without fresh levels, the monthly advanced storyline depends on the monthly trendline as its structural base.

•Multi-Timeframe Confirmation:

A valid monthly advanced breakout must be confirmed by lower timeframes, primarily the daily (and sometimes weekly) breakout signals. This ensures that the shift in trend is supported across multiple timeframes.

•Directional Shifts:

• When a daily bullish trend shifts to bearish via a bearish daily breakout at the monthly trendline, the market is entering a bearish monthly advanced storyline.

• Conversely, when a daily bearish trend shifts to bullish via a bullish daily breakout at the monthly trendline, the market is entering a bullish monthly advanced storyline.

•Consistency:

The advanced approach guarantees that even when fresh monthly SNR levels are depleted, the market’s long-term direction remains clear and actionable.

3. Final Takeaway

•The Monthly Advanced Storyline is the method you use when fresh levels on the monthly chart are no longer present.

•It relies on the monthly trendline as the key reference, with daily (or sometimes weekly) breakouts serving as the confirmation.

•Bearish or bullish daily breakouts at the monthly trendline signal a definitive shift in the monthly advanced storyline.

•This approach ensures you remain aligned with the long-term market direction, even in complex market conditions.

•Definition:

levels or trendlines), price continues in the same direction instead of turning around.

•How It Works:

• For a Bearish Trend:

• Imagine the market was rising. Then, suddenly, a clear bearish breakout occurs (confirmed by a valid QM pattern or a trendline break).

• After that breakout, if the price keeps falling, it shows a Continuation Bearish Storyline.

• For a Bullish Trend:

• Imagine the market was falling. Then, a clear bullish breakout happens (again, confirmed by a valid QM pattern or a trendline break).

• If the price continues rising, that is the Continuation Bullish Storyline.

2. How Do We Confirm a Continuation?

•Using the Classic Approach:

• We look at fresh SNR levels.

• A breakout from a fresh level that does not form a complete reversal (QM pattern) confirms that the trend is continuing.

•Using the Advanced Approach:

• When fresh SNR levels are no longer available, we use the trendline as our guide.

• A breakout on the trendline that does not fully reverse the trend confirms the continuation.

•Extra Confirmation:

• Even if fresh levels exist and line up with the trendline, an early breakout from the trendline can be used to get an extra confirmation that the trend will continue.

3. Why Is This Important?

•Staying on Trend:

• The Continuation Storyline tells you that the market is still in the same trend.

• This helps you avoid trading against the trend and increases your chances of success.

•Clear Signals:

• It gives you a clear signal: if the trend is confirmed by a breakout (either Classic or Advanced) and then continues, you know the momentum is strong.

•Simple Strategy:

• Once you recognize the initial trend shift, you watch for the price to continue in that direction.

• If it does, you have strong confirmation that the trend will persist.

4. Final Takeaway

•For Bearish Trends:

When a daily bullish trend shifts to bearish (confirmed by a breakout from a fresh level or a trendline), and the price continues to fall, this is a Continuation Bearish Storyline.

•For Bullish Trends:

When a daily bearish trend shifts to bullish (confirmed by a breakout from a fresh level or a trendline), and the price continues to rise, this is a Continuation Bullish Storyline.

•Extra Confirmation:

Even if fresh SNR levels are still present and match the trendline, an early breakout from the trendline adds extra confirmation that the trend will continue.

By mastering the Continuation Storyline, you can confidently follow the market's direction, ensuring you trade with the flow and not against it.

2. Middle of the Weekly Storyline

•Bullish Trend:

Once the trend starts, the price continues to move higher by making higher highs and higher lows.

•Bearish Trend:

If the trend is bearish, the price makes lower highs and lower lows.

•Confirmation:

Lower timeframe breakouts—specifically on the 4H chart—confirm that the weekly trend is strong and continues.

3. End of the Weekly Storyline

•Bullish End:

The trend may slow down or reverse as the price approaches a major resistance or shows signs of weakening.

•Bearish End:

The trend may come to a halt or reverse when the price reaches strong support or shows exhaustion.

•Trading Tip:

This phase is where you should take profits or get ready for a possible trend change.

Final Takeaway

When the weekly storyline is in place, you know:

•The start tells you when a new weekly trend begins.

•The middle shows the trend’s strength and gives good entry or re-entry opportunities.

•The end warns you that the trend may be losing momentum and it's time to adjust your trades.

This simple, structured approach helps you see the weekly market as a story—from beginning to end—and makes it easier to trade with confidence.

4.21.Daily Storyline phases: Simple Explanation

The daily storyline shows how price moves throughout the day. It’s divided into three parts—start, middle, and end—and uses fresh daily SNR levels (Classic) or the daily trendline (Advanced) with confirmation from lower timeframes (1H breakouts).

1. Start of the Daily Storyline

•Bullish Daily Start:

The trend begins when price bounces from a fresh daily support or trendline. A bullish breakout, confirmed by a valid QM pattern, signals that buyers are taking control.

•Bearish Daily Start:

The trend begins when price rejects a fresh daily resistance or trendline. A bearish breakout, confirmed by a valid QM pattern, signals that sellers are in control.

•Confirmation:

A breakout on the 1H chart confirms the start of the daily trend.

2. Middle of the Daily Storyline

•Bullish Daily Middle:

The price continues to make higher highs and higher lows, showing strong upward momentum.

•Bearish Daily Middle:

The price continues to form lower highs and lower lows, indicating persistent selling pressure.

•Trading Tip:

This phase offers opportunities to re-enter or add positions, as the trend’s strength is clearly established.

3. End of the Daily Storyline

•Bullish Daily End:

The price nears a major resistance or shows signs of slowing, suggesting the uptrend may reverse or consolidate.

•Bearish Daily End:

The price approaches key support or shows exhaustion, hinting that the downtrend might pause or reverse.

•Trading Tip:

This is the phase to secure profits or prepare for a potential trend change.

Final Takeaway

When the daily storyline is clear:

•Start: Indicates the beginning of a new daily trend.

•Middle: Confirms the trend’s strength.

•End: Warns that the trend may be losing momentum, signaling it might be time to adjust your trades.

This straightforward approach helps you align your trades with the daily market direction using fresh levels or trendlines, with 1H breakouts providing key confirmation.

4.22.The Three Parts of the Weekly + Daily Storyline

Understanding how the weekly and daily storylines interact is crucial for trading. The weekly storyline sets the overall trend for the week, while the daily storyline captures the shorter-term moves within that larger trend. Both can be broken down into three distinct phases: the start, the middle, and the end. This framework helps you time your entries and exits and align your trades with the market’s direction.

1. Weekly Storyline in Three Parts

Start of the Weekly Storyline

The weekly trend begins (bullish or bearish) after a breakout from a key level or trendline.

This sets the overall direction for the week.

Middle of the Weekly Storyline

The trend is in full motion, forming clear higher highs/lows (bullish) or lower highs/lows (bearish).

Traders look for pullbacks or continuation signals here.

End of the Weekly Storyline

The weekly trend slows or reverses near major support/resistance or trendline exhaustion.

This is where you lock profits or watch for a new trend forming.

2. Daily Storyline Nested Within Each Weekly Phase

Inside each weekly phase, the daily chart forms its own start, middle, and end: Start (Daily):

The price reacts at a fresh daily level or daily trendline, indicating a new short-term move.

This can align with the weekly start or happen partway through the weekly trend.

Middle (Daily):

The daily trend gains momentum, making higher highs/lows (bullish) or lower highs/lows (bearish).

Multiple daily cycles can occur within the weekly middle phase.

End (Daily):

The daily move slows near a daily resistance/support or trendline exhaustion.

Even if the weekly trend is ongoing, the daily may pause or briefly reverse before rejoining the bigger move.

3. Why This Matters

1.Clear Structure

1. The weekly chart tells the bigger story, while the daily chart provides mini storylines within each weekly phase.

2.Better Timing

1. By tracking the daily start-middle-end inside the weekly start-middle-end, you can pinpoint more accurate entries and exits.

3.Alignment vs. Conflict

1. If the daily and weekly both point bullish, you have high-confidence setups.

2. If the daily conflicts with the weekly, it may signal a temporary pullback or an early warning that the weekly trend is changing.

4. Putting It All Together

•Start of Weekly → The bigger trend is just beginning. Daily storylines here might also be starting or might have already started.

•Middle of Weekly → The weekly trend is strong. The daily often cycles through multiple smaller moves (higher highs/lows or lower highs/lows), but still follows the weekly direction.

•End of Weekly → The weekly trend weakens or reverses. Daily storylines might show repeated attempts to continue, but ultimately stall.

By combining the three parts of the weekly storyline with the three parts of the daily storyline, you see exactly how short-term moves fit into the bigger picture. This nested approach gives you clarity, confidence, and better timing for every trade.

4.23.Types of Levels in Each Part of the Storyline

The storyline concept in trading is divided into three main parts. The first part marks the initiation of a trend, while the last two parts confirm the continuation of the trend. Each phase contains specific types of levels that play a crucial role in price movement.

1. Levels in the First Part of the Storyline (QM Pattern)

At the beginning of the weekly and daily storyline, the price forms a Quasimodo (QM) pattern, which sets the direction for the upcoming trend. Whether bullish or bearish, this pattern includes specific levels that define its structure.

Bullish QM Levels (Starting a Bullish Storyline)

•Apex: The lowest point before price starts reversing upwards.

•Bullish GAP Level: A price gap that appears as the market shifts from a downtrend to an uptrend.

•Left Shoulder (LS): The level where price previously made a minor high before dropping to form the apex.

•Support Becomes Resistance (SBR): A resistance level formed from a previously strong support level, often acting as a confirmation of the new trend direction.

Bearish QM Levels (Starting a Bearish Storyline)

•Apex: The highest point before price starts reversing downwards.

•Bearish GAP Level: A price gap that appears as the market shifts from an uptrend to a downtrend.

•Left Shoulder (LS): The level where price previously made a minor low before rising to form the apex.

•Resistance Becomes Support (RBS): A support level formed from a previously strong resistance level, confirming the downtrend.

2. Levels in the Last Two Parts of the Storyline (Trend Confirmation) Once the trend has been established by the QM pattern, the last two parts of the storyline focus on trend continuation. These parts contain levels that reinforce the existing trend and provide areas for potential entries.

Levels in a Bullish Trend

•Continuation Apex (C.APEX): The highest point in a retracement before price resumes the uptrend.

•Bullish GAP Level: A price gap that appears within the trend, confirming bullish momentum.

•Resistance Becomes Support (RBS): A previously broken resistance level that now acts as support, reinforcing the continuation of the uptrend.

Levels in a Bearish Trend

•Continuation Apex (C.APEX): The lowest point in a retracement before price resumes the downtrend.

•Bearish GAP Level: A price gap that appears within the trend, confirming bearish momentum.

•Support Becomes Resistance (SBR): A previously broken support level that now acts as resistance, reinforcing t he continuation of the downtrend.

Final Summary

•First part of the storyline: Defines the QM pattern, marking the start of a new trend (bullish or bearish).

•Last two parts of the storyline: Contain continuation levels, confirming that the trend remains valid and is likely to continue.

By recognizing these levels, you can identify trend shifts early and confirm trend strength as the market progresses through each phase of the storyline.

4.24.Rare Case in the QM Pattern: The Early Right Shoulder (ERS) Sometimes, the typical QM pattern doesn’t follow the usual script. In these rare case, before the expected bullish or bearish breakout, you might see a small extra level—a mini A or V shape—that forms. We call this the Early Right Shoulder (ERS).

What is the ERS?

•Definition:

The ERS is an extra level that appears just before the breakout in a QM pattern. It’s like an additional checkpoint that occurs earlier than the standard right shoulder.

•In a Bullish QM Pattern:

• Normally, the pattern forms with a clear low (apex) and then a breakout upward.

• In rare cases, a small V shape (mini low) forms before the breakout. This extra level is the ERS,

•In a Bearish QM Pattern:

• Typically, the pattern shows a high (apex) before a downward breakout.

• Sometimes, a small A shape (mini high) appears before the breakout. This level is the ERS, Key Takeaway

In the rare cases when you see a small A or V shape before a breakout in a QM pattern, that extra level is your Early Right Shoulder (ERS).
