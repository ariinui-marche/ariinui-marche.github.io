5.1 What Are QMS Setups?

Imagine catching a trend right as it’s being written—like getting a sneak peek at the opening chapter of a bestseller.

That’s exactly what our QMS setups do. They’re built around the QM pattern and its levels, which signal the birth of a new trend. Even when the pattern looks a little messy, with multiple gap levels or extra “Early Right Shoulder”

(ERS) levels, each variation has its own precise setup waiting to be learned.

•Based on the QM Pattern:

• The QM pattern signals a fresh trend.

• It comes with specific levels like the apex, gap levels, and ERS (Early Right Shoulder) levels.

•Variability Is Normal:

• The QM pattern might not always look clear.

• Sometimes, you may see more than one gap level or multiple ERS levels, creating a "noisy" pattern.

• Every variation has its own setup, and we will learn exactly how to react in each case.

•Confluence with Trendlines:

• To improve confidence, QMS setups use additional confirmation from trendlines.

• This confluence helps verify the early signal from the QM pattern.

5.2.Dynamic QM Setups in Multi-Timeframe Storylines: Detailed Overview When trading early trend moves, the market sometimes gives multiple QM (Quasimodo) signals. These signals can come in different orders between daily and weekly storylines. Rather than rigid rules, think of these as examples of how the market might unfold—each one presenting a unique opportunity to trade early.

Understanding the Dynamic Nature of QM Setups

In the first part of a trend, a QM pattern signals the start of a new move. This pattern may not always be clean; sometimes you see extra levels such as multiple gap levels or an extra mini level called the Early Right Shoulder (ERS). These variations add layers of opportunity, and each variation can trigger its own setup.

The key is to react to what happens first:

•Sometimes, a QM pattern appears on the 4H timeframe first.

•Other times, the 1H QM pattern emerges earlier.

•Occasionally, you may even see multiple QM patterns in sequence within the same phase, offering additional entries.

5.3.Refinement for QMS Setups: Sharpening Your Entry

Refinement is the key to achieving a sniper entry in QMS setups. When you spot a QM pattern on higher timeframes like 4H or 1H, the apex—where the breakout is expected—may show a long wick. This long wick indicates that price isn’t fully respecting the level, and it’s testing lower timeframes for a more precise entry. We call the levels on these lower timeframes the "freshest levels" , where price finally finds support or resistance.

How It Works

1.4H QM Apex Refinement:

1. Step 1: Look at the 4H QM apex. If it’s a fresh level with a long wick, this signals that the price is not fully respecting the level.

2. Step 2: Check the 1H fresh level at the same area.

3. Step 3: If the 1H level still shows a long wick, move down to the 30M fresh level.

4. Step 4: If the 30M level also has a long wick, finally check the 15M fresh level—this is your last stop for a refined entry.

2.1H QM Apex Refinement:

1. Step 1: Look at the 1H QM apex fresh level.

2. Step 2: If a long wick is present, check the 30M fresh level.

3. Step 3: If the 30M level still has a long wick, then check the 15M fresh level as your final confirmation.

5.4.Handling Dual-Side Rejections with Multi-Timeframe Confluence Sometimes, the market can show conflicting signals—rejections from both sides—at a fresh SNR level. This means you might see both bullish and bearish breakouts occurring at the same time. To navigate these rare cases, we use confluence from multiple timeframes to identify the most powerful direction.

How to Approach Dual-Side Rejections

When you see simultaneous breakouts, follow these steps:

1.Identify the Breakouts:

1. Weekly Levels: Look at 4H breakouts. Both bullish and bearish signals might appear at the same fresh weekly SNR level.

2. Daily Levels: Look at 1H breakouts. You might see conflicting signals at the fresh daily level.

2.Use Trendline Confluence:

1. Check which breakout aligns with the trendline from the advanced storyline.

2. The side that agrees with the higher timeframe (monthly for weekly setups, weekly for daily setups) is the more powerful signal.

3.Consider Overall Market Direction:

1. For weekly setups, if the monthly trend is bullish, favor the bullish breakout—even if a bearish signal is also present.

2. For daily setups, if the weekly trend is bullish, follow the bullish daily breakout.

4.Simplify Your Decision:

1. When conflicting signals occur, the overall higher timeframe direction guides your choice.

2. Use lower timeframe confirmations (4H for weekly, 1H for daily) to fine-tune your entry.

5.7.Failed QM Setups: Catching the Reversal Opportunity

When a new trend is set in motion, it usually begins with a clear QM pattern. Our setups are based on this QM

pattern. But what happens when the QM fails? In other words, when the expected breakout or reversal from the QM pattern does not hold, we have an opportunity to trade the reversal—capturing the move before a new QM

is formed or the overall storyline collapses.

1. Reversal TL-QM Fails Setups

•What It Is:

• This setup occurs when the QM pattern starts the trend but then fails—i.e., the breakout does not hold, leading to a reversal.

•How to Trade It:

• Catch the bearish or bullish move that begins as the QM fails.

• Enter the trade before a new, proper QM pattern forms in the same direction.

• If no new QM forms to confirm the original trend, the entire storyline fails—this setup helps you capitalize on that reversal.

2. QM Failed QM Setup

•What It Is:

• Sometimes, the QM pattern may form without the proper support of a fresh level or trendline, or you might see rejections from both sides with breakouts.

• In such cases, one side’s QM setup fails to hold.

•How to Trade It:

• Identify the failed QM setup when one side’s breakout does not continue.

• Use this failure as an early signal to trade the reversal.

• This setup often appears when there’s a double rejection—when price tries to break out on both sides and one side fails.

5.8.GAP QMS Setups

When it comes to GAP QMS setups, you cannot take a trade directly from the gap level on a 4H or 1H QM pattern.

Instead, GAP setups have their own unique rules that must align with lower timeframe (LTF) levels before you confirm your entry with additional confirmations.

4H GAP QM Setup

•Rule:

The 4H gap level in the 4H QM pattern must align with key LTF levels:

• It should match the 1H gap level.

• It must also align with the 1H Classic Level (an A or V shape level).

• Finally, it must align with the 30M Classic Level (an A or V shape level).

Only when these three conditions are met can you confidently trade using all available confirmation types.

1H GAP QM Setup

•Rule:

The 1H gap level in the 1H QM pattern must align with key LTF levels:

• It should match the 30M Classic Level (an A or V shape level).

• It must also align with the 15M Classic Level (an A or V shape level).

Once these alignment rules are satisfied, you can take your trade confidently with your confirmation signals.

🟢 Take Profit = Destination Checkpoints

•Every fresh level or trendline is like a pit stop along the way.

•Your final destination (TP) depends on whether the daily, weekly, and monthly routes align.

🟢 Storyline Determines the Distance

•If the market is on a smooth road (aligned storyline) → the trade can travel further.

•If the road is bumpy (conflicting storylines) → exit earlier at the safest stop.
