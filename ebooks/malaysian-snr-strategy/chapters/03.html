Time is the most misunderstood tool in trading. Let's use it to our advantage.

3.1.Rejections + Timeframes

Before discussing breakouts, we need to understand rejections in relation to SNR levels. Rejections confirm strong levels and help us determine whether a breakout is valid or false. However, we only focus on rejections for SNR

levels, as trendlines follow a different approach.

There is a direct relationship between rejections and breakouts, depending on whether the price closes beyond the level or not. We won’t go into full details here, as we will break down all the cases when explaining the storyline concept.

1.Rejection with or Without Closing for Breakouts

Rejections behave differently across timeframes. Some higher timeframe rejections without closing can still indicate strength (like on the monthly), while others may suggest uncertainty. The key is to analyze each timeframe's behavior individually.

2.Rejection Structure for SNR Levels:

•Monthly Rejection → Without Monthly Candle Closing (Still strong)

•Weekly Rejection → Without Weekly Candle Closing(Still strong)

•Daily Rejection → With Daily Candle Closing

•4H Rejection → With 4H Candle Closing

•1H Rejection → With 4H Candle Closing

•30Min Rejection → With 30Min Candle Closing

•15Min Rejection → With 15Min Candle Closing

This structured approach ensures we differentiate between temporary rejections and confirmed rejections, helping us correctly interpret breakouts in the next section.

The rejection can occur on any fresh level from the types of SNR levels we have learned. The color of the candle does not matter—what matters is the rejection itself.

3.Rare Cases:

There are instances where a level is not fresh but remains valid only if it has a Confirmation Candle (CC).

The CC is the third candle that forms immediately after the level is created and must be in the same direction as that level:

•On support levels, the CC must be a green (bullish) candle.

•On resistance levels, the CC must be a red (bearish) candle.

A level with a CC acts as a valid level, just like a fresh level. If a rejection occurs on it, it will still be considered valid.

Now that we have thoroughly learned and understood how valid breakouts occur using multiple methods and how rejections must be considered, it’s important to focus on the role of timeframes in confirming their validity.

Every point is aligned with the other, and each concept we’ve covered builds upon the next.

3.2.Breakouts, Rejections, and Timeframes: A General Overview Breakouts are key moments in the market where price surpasses a significant level, indicating a potential shift in trend or continuation of momentum. However, the validity of a breakout heavily depends on timeframe alignment.

Alongside breakouts, rejections also play a crucial role, as they often act as precursors to breakouts or reversals.

Higher vs. Lower Timeframes in Breakouts and Rejections

Higher Timeframe Breakouts & Rejections (HTF)

✅ A breakout on a higher timeframe (e.g., Monthly, Weekly, Daily) carries more weight and indicates a stronger, longer-term market movement.

✅ These breakouts often define major trend shifts and require lower timeframes to confirm price action within that structure.

✅ A rejection at a higher timeframe SNR level suggests a potential reversal, which then translates into price movement on lower timeframes.

.

Lower Timeframe Breakouts & Rejections (LTF)

✅ A breakout on a lower timeframe (e.g., 1H, 30Min, 15Min) happens more frequently but may be less reliable.

✅ These breakouts can show early signs of a potential move but must be validated by the higher timeframe structure.

✅ A rejection on a lower timeframe typically aligns with an HTF structure, meaning a rejection on the 4H could be confirming a move within the weekly structure

3.3.Timeframe for Breakouts & Rejections in SNR Levels

For a breakout to be valid, it must align with the timeframe of the SNR level. Breakouts occurring on lower timeframes should always be validated by the higher timeframe structure to ensure consistency and avoid false signals.

🔹 If a higher timeframe SNR level (e.g., weekly) is broken, confirmation should be observed on a lower timeframe (e.g., daily or 4H).

🔹 If price rejects an HTF level, the reaction will be reflected in the breakouts or rejections of LTF structures.

Timeframe Structure for Breakouts & Rejections

To maintain accuracy, follow this structured approach:

•Monthly SNR → Rejection → Breakout on Daily

•Weekly SNR → Rejection → Breakout on 4H

•Daily SNR → Rejection → Breakout on 1H

•4H SNR → Rejection → Breakout on 1H | 30Min | 15Min

•1H SNR → Rejection → Breakout on 30Min | 15Min

•30Min SNR → Rejection → Breakout on 5Min

•15Min SNR → Rejection → Breakout on 1Min

Core Rule for Valid Breakouts & Rejections

✅ A breakout is only valid if the correct timeframes are identified and followed. Misalignment between the SNR

timeframe and the breakout timeframe can lead to unreliable signals and potential trading mistakes.

✅ A rejection at an HTF level increases the probability of a structured breakout in the lower timeframes.

By understanding the connection between rejections and breakouts across multiple timeframes, traders can make more precise trading decisions and avoid unnecessary risks.

3.4.Timeframe for Breakouts in Trendlines

For a breakout to be valid, it must align with the timeframe of the SNR level or trendline. Breakouts occurring on lower timeframes should always be validated by the higher timeframe structure to ensure consistency and avoid false signals.

If a higher timeframe trendline (e.g., weekly) is broken, confirmation should be observed on a lower timeframe (e.g., daily or 4H). This structured approach ensures that the breakout is significant and not just a minor fluctuation.

Timeframe Structure for Breakouts

To maintain accuracy, follow this structured approach:

•Monthly Trendline → Breakout on Daily

•Weekly Trendline → Breakout on 4H

•Daily Trendline → Breakout on 1H

Core Rule for Valid Breakouts

A breakout is only valid if the correct timeframes are identified and followed. Misalignment between the trendline timeframe and the breakout timeframe can lead to unreliable signals and potential trading mistakes.

3.5.Rare Cases in Multi-Timeframe Breakouts

Breakouts usually follow a clear structure, where a higher timeframe (HTF) level leads to a breakout on a lower timeframe (LTF). But in some rare cases, breakouts don’t follow the usual pattern.

1. When Higher Timeframes Break Out Differently

Most of the time, a breakout on an LTF confirms the movement of the HTF. However, sometimes the breakout happens on a slightly higher LTF than expected because what matters is getting the earliest breakout possible.

For example:

•A monthly SNR level or trendline can lead to a weekly breakout before a daily breakout.

•A weekly SNR level or trendline can lead to a daily breakout before a 4H breakout.

•A daily SNR level or trendline can lead to a 4H breakout before a 1H breakout.

This happens when price rejects strongly on the HTF, triggering an early breakout before the usual lower timeframe confirmation.

2. Why Does This Happen?

This occurs when price, before reaching the HTF SNR level, leaves behind "V" or "A" shape formations with long wicks.

•Inside these long wicks, we often find smaller "V" or "A" shape levels on the LTF.

•These formations act as hidden SNR levels that cause early breakouts on a slightly higher LTF than expected.

Key Takeaways

✅ The goal is to spot early breakouts for better trade entries.

✅ A strong rejection on HTF can cause an earlier breakout than expected.

✅ Wick structures matter—long wicks often contain hidden SNR levels on LTF.

✅ These cases don’t happen often, so traders must confirm with price action before entering trades.

This understanding helps traders stay ahead of price movement and avoid missing key breakouts! 🚀

3.5.Rare LTF Breakouts

For 4H, 1H, 30Min, and 15Min SNR levels, breakouts usually follow the structure of the LTF below them.

However, in rare cases, a breakout can still be valid even if price breaks the last "A" or "V" shape level after the touch.

When Is This Breakout Valid?

•If price touches the SNR level and then breaks the last "A" or "V" shape level, it can still be considered a valid breakout.

•This is similar to the breakout concept with trendlines, where price first interacts with the level before making the actual breakout.

•If we see a QM (Quasimodo) pattern forming after the touch, this confirms the breakout as valid.

Key Takeaways:

✅ LTF breakouts can still be valid even if the last A/V shape level breaks after the touch.

✅ If a QM pattern appears after the touch, it strengthens the breakout confirmation.

✅ This concept follows the same logic as trendline breakouts—price reacts first before breaking the level.

This rare case helps traders avoid confusion and confidently identify breakouts even when they don’t follow the standard pattern! 🚀

3.7.Breakouts on SNR Levels and Trendlines When They Align

In some cases, the price reaches an SNR level that aligns with a trendline. When this happens, we need to understand how to handle breakouts because the breakout concept for SNR levels and trendlines is different.

This rule applies only to the monthly, weekly, and daily timeframes, as these provide stronger confirmation and reduce the risk of false breakouts.

1. The Priority of the Trendline in Breakouts

🔹 Trendlines take priority over SNR levels in breakouts. This means that if the price breaks the trendline first, it is considered a valid breakout, and we do not need to wait for a breakout on the SNR level.

Example:

•If the price gives a 4H breakout on a weekly trendline but has not yet broken the weekly SNR level, we can still consider the breakout valid because the trendline takes priority.

2. Breakouts on SNR Levels Without Trendline Confirmation

🔹 If the breakout happens on the SNR level but not on the trendline, it is still considered a valid breakout.

Why?

•The breakout itself confirms whether a level or trendline is valid. If the price breaks an SNR level, it means the market structure has shifted, even if the trendline remains intact.

Example:

•If the price gives a 1H breakout on a daily SNR level, but the daily trendline remains intact, the breakout is still valid because the level has been broken.

Why This Concept Matters

Understanding how SNR levels and trendlines interact helps traders:

✅ Avoid hesitation when executing trades.

✅ Identify high-probability breakout opportunities.

✅ Reduce false breakouts by focusing on timeframes that provide strong confirmation.

By applying this structured approach to monthly, weekly, and daily timeframes, traders can improve breakout accuracy and make better trading decisions.

3.8. How Many Times Can We Use the Trendline or SNR Level for Breakouts?

When using SNR levels and trendlines for breakouts, there are specific rules on how many times they remain valid before they lose their strength.

1. Using SNR Levels for Breakouts

🔹 First Valid Time: The CC (Confirmation Candle)—this is the first breakout that confirms the level.

🔹 Second Valid Time: A touch followed by a breakout—if the price touches the level again and breaks out, it remains valid.

Beyond these two instances, the level may weaken, and the probability of a strong breakout decreases.

2. Using Trendlines for Breakouts

🔹 Trendlines can remain valid for up to 5 touches.

🔹 In normal cases, the third touch is usually the strongest and most valid for a breakout.

🔹 The 4th and 5th touches can still be valid, but only if they result in a confirmed breakout.

💡 Key Rule: The more times a trendline is touched, the weaker it becomes. However, as long as we get a breakout after the touch, it is still considered valid.

3.9.Drawdown in Trendlines Before Breakouts (Monthly, Weekly, Daily) When the price touches a monthly, weekly, or daily trendline, it can create a drawdown before the actual breakout. This is completely normal and part of the natural price movement.

1. How Drawdown Works on Higher Timeframe Trendlines

🔹 Monthly Trendline → Breakout on Weekly or Daily

🔹 Weekly Trendline → Breakout on Daily or 4H

🔹 Daily Trendline → Breakout on 4H or 1H

The price might reject slightly before the breakout, causing an early breakout on a lower timeframe (LTF) before the higher timeframe (HTF) confirms it.

2. Why Does Drawdown Happen?

🔹 The price often retests or forms a wick rejection before the actual breakout.

🔹 Lower timeframes give early breakouts, showing movement before the HTF breakout.

3. Drawdown Applies to All Major Trendlines

This concept applies specifically to monthly, weekly, and daily trendlines, because they provide stronger confirmations.

💡 Key Takeaways:

✔ A small rejection before a breakout is normal.

✔ An early breakout on a lower timeframe is expected.

✔ Confirm with the HTF before deciding if the breakout is valid.

By understanding this, traders can recognize price movement patterns and avoid false breakout traps.

3.10.Breakouts in Support and Resistance Trendlines: Normal vs. Rare Cases When dealing with trendlines, breakouts usually happen in a predictable manner. However, in rare cases, the price may break out in an unusual way. Understanding both normal and rare breakout behaviors is important for accurate market analysis.

1. Normal Breakout Behavior

✅ Support Trendline → Bullish Breakout Above It

✅ Resistance Trendline → Bearish Breakout Below It

•Support Trendline Breakout: Normally, when the price touches a support trendline, it builds buying pressure.

Once it breaks out, the breakout happens above the trendline, confirming bullish momentum.

•Resistance Trendline Breakout: When the price touches a resistance trendline, it builds selling pressure.

If a breakout happens, it usually occurs below the trendline, confirming a bearish move.

📌 Example:

•A daily support trendline should give a bullish breakout above the trendline.

•A weekly resistance trendline should give a bearish breakout below the trendline.

2. Rare Breakout Cases: Opposite Side Breakouts

🚨 Support Trendline → Bullish Breakout Below It

🚨 Resistance Trendline → Bearish Breakout Above It

In some cases, the price may break out in the opposite direction of the normal behavior:

•Support Trendline Breakout Below: Instead of breaking above, the price dips below the support trendline first, then continues upward.

•Resistance Trendline Breakout Above: Instead of breaking below, the price moves above the resistance trendline before dropping.

📌 Why Does This Happen?

•Price Manipulation: The market sometimes pushes price below or above key levels before reversing.

•Early Breakout Signals: A breakout on a lower timeframe (LTF) may appear first, leading to an unusual breakout structure.

•False Breakouts: The price may temporarily break in the opposite direction before the real breakout occurs.

3.12.Continuation Breakout: A Rare Case

In most cases, we confirm breakouts using the QM pattern, which provides clear structure and confirmation.

However, there are rare situations where the breakout does not form a QM pattern.

This type of breakout is called a Continuation Breakout, which can be classified as:

🔹 Continuation Bullish Breakout (uptrend)

🔹 Continuation Bearish Breakout (downtrend)

1. What is a Continuation Breakout?

A Continuation Breakout happens when the price breaks out without forming a typical QM pattern. This means:

✔ There is no left shoulder level for a bearish breakout.

✔ There is no clear high/low reference before the breakout.

Instead of reversing the trend, this type of breakout reinforces the current trend and confirms that the market will likely continue moving in the same direction.

2. When Do Continuation Breakouts Occur?

Continuation breakouts usually happen inside an already established trend—whether bullish or bearish.

📌 In a Bullish Trend:

•The price breaks resistance and continues higher without forming a typical QM structure.

•No clear left shoulder; just a breakout that follows the existing momentum.

📌 In a Bearish Trend:

•The price breaks support and continues lower without forming a QM structure.

•No clear left shoulder or significant high before the breakout.

3. Why Do Continuation Breakouts Happen?

✅ The market is trending strongly, so there is no need for a reversal structure (QM).

✅ Buyers (in an uptrend) or sellers (in a downtrend) maintain full control.

✅ The price keeps moving in the same direction without hesitation.

3.12.The Ultimate Guide: From SNR Levels to Rare Breakouts

✅ SNR Levels (Support & Resistance Levels)

•Support Levels 🟢 → Areas where price finds demand and bounces up.

•Resistance Levels 🔴 → Areas where price finds supply and moves down.

•Fresh vs. Used SNR Levels:

• Fresh SNR Level: Has never been touched before → more powerful.

• Used SNR Level: Has been touched before → weaker.

✅ Trendlines

•Trendlines track market structure and trend direction.

•The third touch is the most valid one for entries.

•Valid trendlines can hold up to five touches before breaking.

•A breakout on the 4th or 5th touch is still valid.

📌 Golden Rule: Breakouts MUST happen on a valid SNR level or trendline to be trusted!

🔹 2. The Role of Drawdowns Before Breakouts

Before breaking a major trendline, price often creates a drawdown (a pullback or fakeout).

📌 Where Do Drawdowns Happen?

✅ On Monthly, Weekly, and Daily trendlines.

✅ Lower Timeframe (LTF) breakouts happen first before the main breakout on the Higher Timeframe (HTF).

✅ Example:

•If price is about to break a Monthly trendline, we might first see a Weekly or Daily breakout.

•If price is about to break a Weekly trendline, we might first see a Daily or 4H breakout.

•If price is about to break a Daily trendline, we might first see a 4H or 1H breakout.

⚠ Why is this important? → It prevents us from falling for fake breakouts.

The market doesn’t move randomly—it follows a structured storyline of trend shifts.

🔹 For a breakout to confirm a new direction, it must be supported by a valid pattern (QM or Continuation).

✅ Breakout Types That Confirm a Trend Shift:

✅ Breakouts happen on the timeframe of the trend we want to confirm.
