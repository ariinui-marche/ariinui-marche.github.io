1.4.Trendlines:

In the Malaysian SNR strategy, trendlines are straight lines connecting specific points on the chart that form either an A shape or a V shape. These shapes help identify the market's direction and show potential areas where the price might react again.

Simple Steps to Draw Trendlines:

Uptrend Line (support):

Connect two or more points where the price moves upward after forming a V shape (a low point).

The line will slope upward.

Downtrend Line (resistance):

Connect two or more points where the price moves downward after forming an A shape (a high point).

The line will slope downward.

Why Trendlines Are Important:

They show the market’s direction (up or down).

They help you find places where the price might reverse or break through.

Keep your trendlines simple—don’t force them to fit the chart. Use them along with SNR levels for better results!

2.What is the QM Pattern?

The QM pattern (Quasimodo pattern) is a specific price structure that helps confirm strong breakout points.

It consists of a combination of A shapes and V shapes that clearly define key price levels.

The QM pattern shows that the market has created a clear break of structure, signaling a potential reversal or continuation.

3.Key Levels in the QM Pattern:

Bearish QM Pattern – Key Levels

High: The first A shape (peak) created by the price before it reverses downward.

Low: The first V shape (bottom) created by the price after the first high.

Higher High (HH): The second A shape, which forms a higher peak than the first high. This shows temporary strength in the trend.

Lower Low (LL): The second V shape, which breaks below the first low. This break confirms the QM pattern and signals a valid breakout.

Bullish QM Pattern – Key Levels

1.Low (L):

1. The first V shape (bottom) formed by the price before reversing upward.

2.High (H):

1. The first A shape (peak) created after the low, marking the initial resistance.

3.Lower Low (LL):

1. The second V shape (bottom), which dips below the first low.

2. This break creates a trap and strengthens the potential bullish reversal.

4.Higher High (HH):

1. The second A shape (peak), which breaks above the first high.

2. This confirms the QM pattern and signals a valid bullish breakout.

4.How to Identify a Valid QM Pattern:

1.Look for the four levels (High, Low, Higher High, and Lower Low).

2.Check that the breakout breaks the structure (the lower low for bearish, or higher high for bullish).

3.Ensure the breakout candle has a strong body to confirm the pattern.

Using the QM pattern ensures you focus only on breakouts that have high probability, reducing false signals and improving your trading accuracy.

2.5.Understanding Market Trends

the market moves through three primary patterns:

Bullish Trend: A phase where the price consistently moves upward, breaking resistance levels and respecting support levels.

Bearish Trend: A phase where the price consistently moves downward, breaking support levels and respecting resistance levels.

Consolidation: A range-bound phase where the price fluctuates between a support level and a resistance level, indicating market indecision.

The breakout concept in MSNR is used to determine when the market transitions between trends—either from bearish to bullish or vice versa. This is critical for identifying potential trading opportunities and staying aligned with the overall market direction.

In MSNR, we focus primarily on the bullish and bearish trends and use tools like the Quasimodo (QM) pattern to recognize key reversal points and breakout scenarios effectively.

For now, let’s concentrate on understanding the types of trends as they apply to the MSNR strategy.

2.QM Pattern + Trends

The QM pattern has two parts:

•Bearish QM:

•The first part is a segment of the bullish trend.

•The second part is a segment of the bearish trend.

When the second part breaks the first part, it confirms the transition from a bullish trend to a bearish trend. The second part is what validates the breakout (external breakout).

If the breakout happens inside the structure, it is not a valid breakout (internal breakout).

•Bullish QM:

•The first part is a segment of the bearish trend.

•The second part is a segment of the bullish trend.

When the second part breaks the first part, it confirms the transition from a bearish trend to a bullish trend. The second part is what validates the breakout (external breakout).

If the breakout happens inside the structure, it is not a valid breakout (internal breakout). .

3.Trends + SNR Levels + QM

For a Bullish Trend:

We need to identify the last LOW (V-shape level) before the touch. This will be our valid level to break by the second part of the QM pattern, confirming the switch from bullish to bearish For a Bearish Trend:

We need to identify the last HIGH (A-shape level) before the touch. This will be our valid level to break by the second part of the QM pattern, confirming the switch from bearish to bullish.

4.Trends + Trendlines + QM

To switch from a bullish trend to a bearish trend, we need to see a clear breakout of part 2 through part 1 in the QM pattern.

For the opposite transition, from a bearish trend to a bullish trend, we need to see a clear breakout of part 2 through part 1 in the QM pattern in the opposite direction.

2.6.The Difference Between Breakouts on Trendlines and Breakouts on SNR Levels 1.Breakout on SNR Levels

•The breakout must happen after the last touch on the SNR level.

•If a QM pattern forms after the last touch, it is not valid for confirming the breakout.

•This means the price must respect the SNR level before breaking it.

•If the breakout happens before the last touch, the level is still in play, making it unreliable.

2.Breakout on Trendlines (TL)

•A QM pattern after the touch on the trendline is still valid for a breakout.

•Unlike SNR levels, price can interact with the trendline and still break out correctly.

•Early breakouts on trendlines are also valid, as trendlines adjust with market movement.

3.Main Difference

•For SNR levels: The breakout must come after the last touch, and any QM pattern after that is invalid.

•For trendlines: The breakout is valid even if a QM pattern forms after the touch, and early breakouts are okay.

Levels in a Bearish Trend

•Bearish GAP Level: A price gap that appears within the trend, confirming bearish momentum.

•Support Becomes Resistance (SBR): A previously broken support level that now acts as resistance, reinforcing t he continuation of the downtrend.

Final Summary
